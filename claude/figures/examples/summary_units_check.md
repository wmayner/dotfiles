# Fresh-reader test of summary_units.png

Each round, one new subagent (Opus) with no context was given only the PNG, no caption, and asked: what was done, what was found, and what was unclear or could be read more than one way. 20 October 2026.

Intended reading: in hand-built networks with a known circuit, keeping the circuit as neurons and ablating the rest preserves the output when the true units are neurons, and loses it when the true units are directions spread over all neurons.

## Round 1

Done: "built small networks with a known circuit planted in them. In the top row the circuit uses individual neurons. In the bottom row it uses directions that cut across many neurons. In both, they kept only the neurons treated as the circuit, ablated the rest, and compared the circuit's output with the full network's."

Found: "When the true units are neurons, the circuit alone reproduces the full network's output. When they are directions, keeping neurons breaks the circuit and the output flips."

Unclear: how the bottom row's kept neurons were chosen; what the bars measure; real data or schematic; "the horizontal orange line between the two arrows in the bottom left"; whether "Keep it as neurons" names the method or a constraint.

Change: removed the line drawn between the two directions, which read as an unexplained mark.

## Round 2

Done: "two small networks, each with a planted circuit whose true structure is known ... a circuit is pulled out at the level of neurons: every neuron outside the chosen set is ablated. Then the output of the full network is compared with the output of the circuit alone."

Found: "When the true units are neurons, the circuit reproduces the output. When the true units are directions, the circuit gets the output wrong."

Unclear: the bottom row's kept neurons "look copied from the top row"; the grey dashed arrows; the bars; schematic or measured.

Change: kept a different set of neurons in the bottom row (the same count), so it no longer looks copied.

## Round 3 (final image)

Done: "A known circuit is planted in two small networks. In the top network the circuit's units are single neurons. In the bottom network they are directions spread across many neurons. Both are then analyzed by a method that describes the circuit in terms of whole neurons and ablates every other neuron. Last, the output of the full network is compared with the output of the circuit alone."

Found: "When the true units are neurons, the circuit alone gives the same output as the full network. When they are directions, it does not; the output distribution flips."

Unclear: how the bottom row's neurons were chosen; the bars have no axis; method or constraint; measured or schematic.

All three readings got the setup and the finding right. What remains unclear (how the neurons were chosen, what the bars measure, whether the result is measured) is what the caption answers. No reader saw that the result in the bottom row holds by construction, or that it was compared with random neuron sets; both are in the caption.
